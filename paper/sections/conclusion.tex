\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We present \FenceDD, a verified library of double-double arithmetic.  For each
ordinary C call that returns normally, we prove that the result satisfies the
stated error bound and is a double-double.

Our numerical analysis extends the results of \citet{Joldes2017}, mechanized
in FLX by \citet{Rideau2022}, which assume that underflow and overflow do not
occur.  Building on results for error-free transformations near
underflow \citep{BoldoDaumas2003,Boldo2017}, we carry the analysis through the
double-word algorithms and prove their guarantees under IEEE~754
semantics.  Addition retains a relative error bound proportional to the square
of the unit roundoff even when gradual underflow occurs.  Multiplication and
division instead satisfy mixed bounds that combine such a relative term with
an absolute term proportional to the smallest positive representable value.
They therefore provide roughly twice the relative precision where the
relative term dominates and a small absolute error near the smallest
representable values.

For each double-word operation, the default and strong wrappers provide
the same contract on normal return.  The strong wrapper uses a
strong check to establish result finiteness before execution.
The default wrapper uses a weak check before execution and checks
result finiteness afterward, allowing its runtime checks to succeed for more
inputs.  Each error-free transformation has one wrapper.

Across \RQOneNormalReturnCount{} results produced by accepted calls, the largest
fractions of the proved bounds are \RQOneRelativeMaximumRatio{} in
the relative region and \RQOneAbsoluteMaximumRatio{} in the absolute region.
On inputs associated with calls rejected by runtime checks in the default and
strong wrappers, using QD we obtain a finite double-double result whose
observed error is no greater than the
corresponding \FenceDD{} error bound in
\RQTwoDefaultQDWithinPercent{} and \RQTwoStrongQDWithinPercent{} of cases,
respectively; on inputs associated with rejected calls to the wrappers
for \FastTwoSum{} and \FastTwoMult{}, we obtain exact reconstructions using
QD in only
\RQTwoFastSumExactPercent{} and \RQTwoFastMultExactPercent{} of cases.  The
wrappers take
\RQThreeMinimumKernelRatio{}--\RQThreeMaximumKernelRatio{} times as long as
their unchecked kernels and
\RQThreeMinimumQDRatio{}--\RQThreeMaximumQDRatio{} times as long as QD.
Together, these results show that proved numerical guarantees for double-word
arithmetic can be provided through a C interface over a useful range of
inputs.
