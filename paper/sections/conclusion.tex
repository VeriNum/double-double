\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We present \FenceDD, a verified C library for double-double arithmetic.
Through its ordinary C interface, a double-word operation that returns
normally produces a double-double satisfying its proved error bound, while an
error-free transformation that returns normally reconstructs its exact result.

Our numerical analysis extends the results of \citet{Joldes2017}, mechanized
by \citet{Rideau2022}, from an unbounded exponent range to IEEE~754
arithmetic.  Building on results for individual error-free transformations near
underflow \citep{BoldoDaumas2003,Boldo2017}, we carry underflow errors through
the complete double-word operations and identify conditions that ensure finite
results.  Addition retains a relative
error bound proportional to the square of the unit roundoff under gradual
underflow.  Multiplication and division instead require mixed bounds whose
absolute terms account for errors near the smallest representable values.

In our evaluation, the largest observed errors reached $0.87$ of their proved
bound when the relative term dominated and $0.56$ when the absolute term
dominated.  For inputs on which runtime checks stopped calls to double-word
operations, QD produced a result satisfying the corresponding numerical
properties in at most $6.1\%$ of cases; exact reconstruction was similarly
rare for error-free transformation calls stopped by runtime checks.  Relative to the
unchecked kernels, the wrappers added $2.5$--$11$ nanoseconds per call.
Together, these results show that proved numerical guarantees for double-word
arithmetic can be provided through an ordinary C interface at modest absolute
cost.
